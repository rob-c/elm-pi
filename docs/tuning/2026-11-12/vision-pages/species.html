Frog Species

Discover the incredible diversity of frogs from around the world

Exploring Frog Diversity

With over 7,000 known species, frogs represent one of the most diverse groups of vertebrates on Earth. Each species has evolved unique adaptations to thrive in its specific habitat, from tropical rainforests to arid deserts.

This gallery showcases some of the most fascinating frog species, organized by their primary habitats and characteristics.

Tree Frogs

Tree frogs are arboreal amphibians adapted for life in the canopy. They possess specialized toe pads that allow them to climb and cling to smooth surfaces like leaves and branches.

Red-Eyed Tree Frog

Scientific Name: Agalychnis callidryas

Habitat: Tropical rainforests of Central America, from Mexico to Colombia

Size: 1-2.75 inches (2.5-7 cm)

Unique Features: Vibrant red eyes, blue and yellow striped sides, bright orange toes. Nocturnal and known for their striking appearance.

White's Tree Frog

Scientific Name: Litoria caerulea

Habitat: Australia and New Guinea

Size: Up to 5 inches (12.5 cm)

Unique Features: Also called the "dumpy tree frog," they're docile, hardy, and popular as pets. They secrete a substance with antibacterial properties.

Gray Tree Frog

Scientific Name: Hyla versicolor

Habitat: Eastern United States and Canada

Size: 1.25-2 inches (3-5 cm)

Unique Features: Can change color from gray to green or brown based on environment and temperature. Their trilling call can last up to 3 seconds.

Poison Dart Frogs

Poison dart frogs are among the most colorful and toxic animals on Earth. Indigenous people historically used their secretions to poison blowdart tips for hunting.

Golden Poison Frog

Scientific Name: Phyllobates terribilis

Habitat: Pacific coast of Colombia

Size: Up to 2 inches (5 cm)

Unique Features: The most toxic frog species. A single frog carries enough batrachotoxin to kill 10 grown men. Bright golden-yellow coloration warns predators.

Blue Poison Dart Frog

Scientific Name: Dendrobates tinctorius "azureus"

Habitat: Suriname and Brazil

Size: 1.5-1.75 inches (4-4.5 cm)

Unique Features: Brilliant blue with black spots. In captivity, they lose toxicity due to diet changes. Highly social and active during the day.

Strawberry Poison Frog

Scientific Name: Oophaga pumilio

Habitat: Central America (Nicaragua, Costa Rica, Panama)

Size: 0.7-1 inch (1.8-2.5 cm)

Unique Features: Extremely variable coloration - red, blue, green, or yellow morphs exist. Females carry tadpoles to water-filled bromeliads and feed them unfertilized eggs.

⚠️ Toxicity Note

Poison dart frogs in the wild derive their toxins from their diet of ants, mites, and other small insects. Captive-bred specimens fed different diets are not toxic, making them safe to handle (though it's still not recommended).

Bullfrogs & Large Aquatic Species

These powerful swimmers dominate freshwater ecosystems. They're characterized by their large size, aquatic lifestyle, and distinctive deep calls.

American Bullfrog

Scientific Name: Lithobates catesbeianus

Habitat: North America (introduced worldwide)

Size: 3.5-6 inches (9-15 cm), up to 1.5 lbs (0.7 kg)

Unique Features: Largest frog in North America. Voracious predator eating insects, fish, birds, and even other frogs. Deep, resonant call sounds like "jug-o-rum."

Goliath Frog

Scientific Name: Conraua goliath

Habitat: Cameroon and Equatorial Guinea

Size: Up to 12.5 inches (32 cm), over 7 lbs (3.3 kg)

Unique Features: The world's largest frog species. Endangered due to habitat loss and overharvesting for food. Builds nests by clearing vegetation in streams.

African Clawed Frog

Scientific Name: Xenopus laevis

Habitat: Sub-Saharan Africa

Size: 2-5 inches (5-12 cm)

Unique Features: Fully aquatic with webbed feet and clawed toes. Used extensively in biological research. Can live over 15 years in captivity.

Unique & Unusual Species

Some frogs have evolved extraordinary adaptations that set them apart from their relatives.

Glass Frog

Scientific Name: Family Centrolenidae

Habitat: Central and South American rainforests

Size: 0.8-3 inches (2-7.5 cm)

Unique Features: Translucent skin on their undersides reveals internal organs, including the beating heart. Excellent camouflage against predators.

Pacman Frog

Scientific Name: Ceratophrys ornata

Habitat: Argentina, Uruguay, Brazil

Size: 4-7 inches (10-18 cm)

Unique Features: Round body and enormous mouth (hence the nickname). Ambush predator that buries itself and lunges at prey. Popular exotic pet.

Surinam Toad

Scientific Name: Pipa pipa

Habitat: Northern South America

Size: 4-8 inches (10-20 cm)

Unique Features: Females carry fertilized eggs embedded in skin on their backs. Young emerge from pores in the mother's back. Flat, star-shaped body perfect for camouflage.

🌍 Conservation Status

Approximately 40% of frog species are threatened with extinction. Habitat destruction, climate change, pollution, and the chytrid fungus are major threats. Many species featured here are protected by conservation programs.
